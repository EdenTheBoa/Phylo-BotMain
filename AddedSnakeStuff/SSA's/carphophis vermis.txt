The Western Wormsnake *Carphophis vermis* is a small (record 39.1 cm), harmless, dipsadine snake found that occurs west of the Mississippi River. This snake generally inhabits woodlands and edge habitat along streams through prairies. Even more so than their counterparts the Eastern Wormsnake *Carphophis ameonus*, they are rarely encountered due to their secretive nature and semifossorial habits. Wormsnakes spend most of their time underground as they are sensitive to desiccation, (drying out). These snakes feed primarily on earthworms but will also eat other small, soft-bodied invertebrates. 

Western Wormsnakes are typically patternless, with a brown dorsum and white or sometimes vividly pink underside, which can be more pronounced in hatchlings. They have smooth scales and a small, slender head. The tail is short and terminates in a sharp, spine-like scale. This is a docile species that rarely attempts to bite, more likely to emit a foul-smelling discharge called “musk” and attempt to escape. If seized, they may also jab their captor with their tail spine, which is harmless.
