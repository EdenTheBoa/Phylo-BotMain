Tamaulipan Hook-nosed Snakes *Ficimia streckeri* are small (18-28cm, up to 48cm) colubrid snakes that range from southern Texas south into northern Veracruz, MX, from near sea level to 1,500m.   They inhabit humid microhabitat within semidesert scrub and riparian floodplain, especially along waterbodies, ditches, farmland, and residential yards.

Nocturnal and semifossorial in habit, *F. streckeri* spend most of the day underground or under ground cover.  They are most typically encountered at night, especially during or after rainy weather.  Their diet is composed of spiders, centipedes, and other invertebrates.

Short and moderately stout in build, *F. streckeri* have a small head which is indistinct at the neck.  The dorsal scales are smooth, moderately glossy, and arranged in 17 rows at midbody.  [The nasal scale is fused with the anterior portion of the first supralabial scale.  The elongate rostral scale is in contact with the frontal scale posteriorly and flat or concave except at the very front, where it tapers to a fine, upturned point](https://snakeevolution.org/Fstreckeri.jpg).  This inspires the common name "Hook-nosed Snake".  There is a single postocular scale.  They are greyish, tan, brown, or olive above with dark spots or narrow transverse bands that are about one scale long.

Other snakes may be confused with *F. streckeri*.  Brown Hook-nosed Snakes *F. olivacea* are uniform brownish above with no dorsal pattern.  Blotched Hook-nosed Snakes *F. publia* and Hardy's Hook-nosed Snakes *F. hardyi* have larger dorsal markings which are 2-4 scales long and usually two postocular scales.  [The unique shape and positioning of the rostral scale will differentiate *F. streckeri* from all other snakes in range](https://snakeevolution.org/Fstreckeri.jpg), which either either lack the upturned snout and/or have rostral scales which are separated from the frontal scale by the prefrontals, internasals, or both.
