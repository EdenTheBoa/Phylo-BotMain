Bar-necked Keelbacks *Fowlea schnurrenbergeri* are medium sized (~60cm, record 90cm), Old World natricine snakes that range from southeastern Pakistan east through northern India into southern Nepal and western Bangladesh.  Semiaquatic in habit, they are reliant upon water sources such as ponds, lakes, and beels, where they shelter within vegetation such as water hyacinth.  Prey consists primarily of frogs and fish.

When disturbed *F. schnurrenbergeri* attempt to flee with fast, rather jumpy movements.  If cornered or approached, they sometimes flatten the head and/or body to make themselves appear larger, bite or pretend to bite, and release a foul smelling musk from the vent; they are reportedly more timid than their relative *F. piscator*.

Bar-necked Keelbacks have keeled dorsal scales which are arranged in 19 rows at midbody.  There are usually 9 supralabials, with the 4th and 5th contacting the eye.  The dorsum is olive-brown with 5-6 rows of large, [dark checkerboard spots](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/221537315?size=large) which get smaller posteriorly.  A pair of dark, vertical bars often merge transversely across the neck.  The closely related and similar looking Checkered Keelback *F. piscator* overlaps broadly in range, but has a larger head which is more distinct at the neck, and when a dark marking is present on the neck, it is shaped like a "V".
