Hump-nosed Pitvipers *Hypnale hypnale* are small (29-38cm, up to 55cm) pitvipers that range through the Western Ghats of India south into Sri Lanka, from near sea level to 1,525m.  They utilize a variety of forested habitat, where they prefer cool and humid microhabitat, and they can sometimes be found in adjoining areas, especially agricultural zones.

Hump-nosed Pitvipers are a dangerously venomous species and should only be observed from a safe distance.  Bites sometimes occur when an animal is accidentally encroached upon or inadvertently touched, but intentional attempts to kill, capture, prod, or handle the snake dramatically increase a person's odds of being bitten.  **The best way to avoid being bitten is to leave the snake alone.**

Primarily nocturnal and terrestrial in habit, *H. hypnale* are nonetheless good climbers and can sometimes be found in shrubs and other low vegetation.  More frequently, they hide under or at the base of logs, rocks, or among leaf litter.  This, along with their cryptic pattern and coloration, reduces their odds of being detected by one of their many predators while also offering them a good position from which to ambush their own prey.  Lizards, small rodents, and frogs make up most of their diet.

Hump-nosed Vipers are moderately stout in build. The head is very distinct at the neck, and a sharply angular ridge runs from the eye toward the snout.  The common name is derived from the [upturned snout](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/169461340?size=large), the top of which is covered in a series of small, irregular scales.  The dorsal scales are weakly keeled and arranged in 17 rows at midbody.  Coloration and pattern are variable.  The ground color is always brownish, but can be light to very dark, and shades vary from tan to golden brown, grey-brown, reddish brown, chocolate brown, or dark brown.  The upper dorsum is patterned with [two rows of dark markings](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/56608675?size=large) which may be triangular, rounded, or irregular in shape.  Additional grey, brown, or reddish brown markings are sometimes present along the lateral surface but are usually indistinct.  The combination of small size and upturned snout will reliably differentiate *H. hypnale* from other vipers that overlap in range.
