Black Swampsnakes *Liodytes pygaea* are small (25-38cm, up to 56cm) New World natricine snakes that range from eastern North Carolina south along the coastal plain into Georgia, extreme southern Alabama, and through Florida, from near sea level up to 150m.  They inhabit calm, well vegetated, freshwater habitat, including ponds, lakes, swamps, marshes, sluggish streams, and ditches.  Brackish wetlands are occasionally utilized as well.

Secretive in habit, *L. pygaea* are rarely observed even in areas where they are common.  They are active day and night, foraging amongst aquatic vegetation.  The prey primarily upon aquatic salamanders and salamander larvae, but other amphibians, small fish, and leeches are also consumed.

[Black Swampsnakes are black or dark brown above and bright red bellow](https://www.flickr.com/photos/tamers1/14750018926/).  Their dorsal scales are smooth and arranged in 17 rows across midbody.  Light colored, longitudinal lines down the center of the scales might resemble keels, especially along the lower scale rows.  They are unlikely to be confused with other snakes, but the superficially similar Red-Bellied Snakes *Storeria occipitomaculata* have strongly keeled dorsal scales which are arranged in 15 rows across the body, a light spot just behind the head, and a distinctively short, blunt snout.
