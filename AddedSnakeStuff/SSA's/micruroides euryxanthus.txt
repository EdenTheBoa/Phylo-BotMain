Sonoran Coralsnakes *Micruroides euryxanthus* are small (28-36cm, up to 62cm) elapid snakes that range through the southern and central Arizona east into southwestern New Mexico, and south into Jalisco, MX from near sea level to 1,800m in elevation. They inhabit arid and semi-arid regions, particularly those with rocky soils, including riparian corridors, rocky slopes, plains, montane woodland, scrub, desert, tropical dry forest and agricultural areas. 

Primarily nocturnal and semi-fossorial in habit, *M. euryxanthus* spends the majority of its life under leaf litter, rocks, or underground. They are mainly active during the summer months and are most commonly seen from June through September, often after rainfall. *Rena* Threadsnakes, *Tantilla* Black-Headed Snakes, and other small species of snakes and smooth scaled lizards make up the majority of these snakes' diet. 

*Micruroides euryxanthus* is a dangerously venomous species and should only be observed from a safe distance. Common defensive tactics include hiding the head within the coils of itself, using the tail as a “false head”, [raising of the midbody off the ground](https://imgur.com/a/lZF3cgm) and [emitting a “popping” sound from the cloaca](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K8VNsVhIon8). They are not aggressive and only bite when they feel they are in danger. Bites most commonly occur when a human attempts to kill, capture, or otherwise intentionally handle the snake. **The best way to avoid being bitten is to leave the snake alone.**

Sonoran Coralsnakes are slender in build with a short tail, a small head which is mostly indistinct at the neck, and small eyes. The dorsal scales are smooth and arranged in 15 rows at midbody (17 dorsal scale rows at the neck, 15 rows posteriorly). [The striking, tricolored dorsal pattern consists of red, black, and white-yellow bands throughout the length of the body](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/146734148?size=large). The anal and subcaudal scales are divided. They are the only member of the monotypic genus *Micruroides* and are closely related (sister) to the more widespread *Micrurus* Coralsnakes.
