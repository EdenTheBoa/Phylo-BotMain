Mexican Vine Snakes *Oxybelis aeneus* are long but slender (record 152.4cm) snakes with smooth or weekly keeled scales found a few miles north of the border in Arizona through Mexico to South America and Brazil. This species likes areas with lots of brush along hillsides and canyon corridors. They use grooved rear fangs to deliver a venom toxic to their lizard prey which may also have a moderate effect on humans- see the !harmless bot reply. 

In the tropics this is one of the most abundant snakes. Vine Snakes aren't easily confused with other species; they have long, thin heads, bodies and tails.

This species name is still under revision - see the phylogeography link below. It will be updated when the complex is fully named and revised, but because of where the holotype was described, Oxybelis aeneus will be restricted to one of the species found in the Amazon and the vinesnakes in Arizona and Mexico will get a new name.
