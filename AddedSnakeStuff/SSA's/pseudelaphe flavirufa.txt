Yellow-Red Ratsnakes *Pseudelaphe flavirufa* are medium-large (120-150cm, up to 176cm) colubrid snakes that range from Tamaulipas MX south and west into Honduras and Nicaragua (see range map for details), from near sea level to 1,500m.  They utilize a wide variety of habitat, including rainforest, dry woodland, savanna, scrubland, grassland, and agricultural zones.  A closely related species, the Yucatan Ratsnake *P. phaescens*, replaces them in the northern part of the Yucatan pensinula.

Primarily nocturnal, *P. flavirufa* are usually observed at night while foraging or crossing roads.  Terrestrial in habit, they are good climbers nonetheless and can sometimes be found up in shrubs and trees, and sometimes enter buildings.  The bulk of their diet is comprised of rodents, but lizards, bats, and small birds are commonly consumed.

Yellow-Red Ratsnakes are moderately slender and muscular in build.  [The head](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/216561843?size=large) is distinct at the neck and moderately elongate with large eyes that are in contact with 3 of the usually 9-10 supralabial scales.  A single loreal, 1-2 preoculars, and two postocular scales are present.  The dorsal scales are mostly smooth, with weak keels present only along the middorsal rows, and arranged in 25-31 rows at midbody.  

The pattern of *P. flavirufa* consists of 33-46 [red](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/59539771?size=large) to [brown](https://www.flickr.com/photos/jmichels/9122471026/) dorsal blotches on a ground color of cream or tan to greyish-brown.  The blotches have a well defined black border, and [sometimes merge with each other](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/95490006?size=large), imparting a "wavy" or "zig-zag" look.  Like other North American ratsnakes, the body has a unique cross-sectional shape.  It is slightly compressed laterally, rounded at the top, and angular at the junction of the belly and the sides, both of which are almost flat.  This shape is best likened to a [loaf of bread](https://decoratedtreats.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/homemade-white-bread-loaf-feature-500x500.jpg), a [train tunnel](https://thumbs.dreamstime.com/b/old-train-tunnel-railway-mountain-76646034.jpg), or a [rural mailbox](https://i5.walmartimages.com/asr/a845f7ea-2173-49af-a559-70b2145f8704_1.75c8e9b5192cd187148446aa89e91821.jpeg).

Yellow-red Ratsnakes are most likely to be confused with close relatives.  Yucatan Ratsnakes *P. phaescens* have fewer than 33 dorsal blotches before the tail, which are proportionally longer and also concave at the ends.  Green Ratsnakes *Senticolis triaspis* have a proportionally smaller eye, usually only eight supralabial scales, and only two supralabial scales usually in contact with the eye.  Other common names for *P. flavirufa* include "Tropical Ratsnake", "Mexican Ratsnake", and "Red-blotched Ratsnake".
