Yucatan Ratsnakes *Pseudelaphe phaescens* are medium-large (110-130cm, up to 160cm) colubrid snakes that are endemic to the northern part of the Yucatan Peninsula in Mexico.  They mainly inhabit humid forest, dry woodland, and scrubland.  A closely related species, the Yellow-red Ratsnake *P. flavirufa*, replaces them in the southern part of the Yucatan Peninsula and surrounding areas.

Primarily nocturnal, *P. phaescens* are usually observed at night while foraging or crossing roads.  Terrestrial in habit, they are good climbers nonetheless and often ascend shrubs, trees, rocks, and walls, and sometimes enter caves and buildings.  Their diet consists largely of rodents and bats, but lizards and small birds are probably also consumed.

Yucatan Ratsnakes are moderately slender and muscular in build.  [The head](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/80273864?size=large) is distinct at the neck and moderately elongate with large eyes that are in contact with three of the usually 9-10 supralabial scales.  A single loreal, one preocular, and two postocular scales are present.  The dorsal scales are weakly keeled at middorsum, smooth laterally, and arranged in 27-31 rows at midbody.

The pattern of *P. phaescens* consists of 27-31 reddish or brown [dorsal blotches](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/75369507?size=large) on a ground color of yellowish to grey.  The blotches have a well defined black border and at least some of them are concave at the ends.  Like other North American ratsnakes, the body has a unique cross-sectional shape.  It is slightly compressed laterally, rounded at the top, and angular at the junction of the belly and the sides, both of which are almost flat.  This shape is best likened to a [loaf of bread](https://decoratedtreats.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/homemade-white-bread-loaf-feature-500x500.jpg), a [train tunnel](https://thumbs.dreamstime.com/b/old-train-tunnel-railway-mountain-76646034.jpg), or a [rural mailbox](https://i5.walmartimages.com/asr/a845f7ea-2173-49af-a559-70b2145f8704_1.75c8e9b5192cd187148446aa89e91821.jpeg).

Yucatan Ratsnakes are most likely to be confused with close relatives.  Yellow-red Ratsnakes *P. flavirufa* have at least 33 dorsal blotches before the tail, which are proportionally smaller and of which few or none are concave at the ends.  Green Ratsnakes *Senticolis triaspis* have a proportionally smaller eye, usually only eight supralabial scales with only two in contact with the eye, and usually 45+ dorsal blotches before the tail.
