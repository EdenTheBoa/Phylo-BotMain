Baird's Patchnose Snakes *Salvadora bairdi* are medium sized (70-100cm, up to 113cm) colubrid snakes endemic to Mexico, from eastern Sonora and western Chihuahua south to Jalisco and southeast across the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt and Mexican Plateau to Veracruz, from 1000m above sea level up to 3,200m.  They inhabit open, grassy or scrubby areas within or along the edges of tropical dry forest, montane forest and woodland, and savanna.

Primarily diurnal in habit, *S. bairdi* may become crepuscular during hot weather.  Active and alert, they rely on their eyesight and speed to capture prey and evade predators.  Though mainly terrestrial, they are strong climbers and will occasionally ascend shrubs and low trees to bask or to forage for their prey, which consists of lizards, snakes, rodents, and reptile eggs.

Baird's Patchnose Snakes are fairly slender in build.  [The common name is inspired by the distinctively enlarged rostral scale](https://snakeevolution.org/Sbairdi.jpg), which apparently aids in excavating loose soil to locate prey.  The dorsal scales are smooth and arranged in 17 rows at midbody.  [There are eight supralabial scales and two (usually 4,5) contact the eye.  A single loreal scale is present and there are two preoculars.](https://snakeevolution.org/Sbairdi.jpg)  The anal scale is divided.  The ground color is pale grey, cream, tan, or yellowish.  A pale tan, yellow, orange, or cream middorsal stripe is widest at the nape but narrows posteriorly.  [This pale stripe is narrower than a pair of dark, dorsolateral stripes which border it on both sides.  These dorsolateral stripes terminate anteriorly at the neck.  An additional pair of dark lateral stripes are much thinner and positioned on the third dorsal scale row and sometimes the lower edges of the fourth.](https://snakeevolution.org/Sbairdi.jpg)  The crown of the head ranges from the same color to much darker than the middorsal stripe, and the two are sometimes separated by a dark patch.

Other snakes may be confused for *S. bairdi*.  All other *Salvadora* Patchnose Snakes the overlap or approach the range of *S. bairdi* have dark stripes which run toward or through the snout except for the Mexican Patchnose Snake *S. mexicana*, which has nine supralabials, supralabials 4-6 in contact with the eye, and only one preocular.  *Thamnophis* Gartersnakes have strongly keeled dorsal scales, lack the enlarged rostral scale, and have an undivided anal scale.  *Masticophis* Whipsnakes lack both the enlarged rostral scale and the pale middorsal stripe.
