Big Bend Patchnose Snakes *Salvadora deserticola* are medium sized (61-81 cm, up to 114cm) colubrid snakes that range from southeastern Arizona and southern New Mexico south into western Texas and northwestern Coahuila, Chihuahua, and Sonora, and south along the Pacific versant into northern Nayarit, from near sea level up to 1,830m.  They inhabit a wide variety of dry terrain with rocky or sandy soils, including desert, desertscrub, scrubland, tropical dry forest, montane woodland, plateaus, arroyos, and rocky slopes.

Primarily diurnal in habit, *S. deserticola* may become crepuscular during hot weather.  Though mainly terrestrial, they are good climbers and will occasionally ascend into shrubs and low trees to forage or bask.  They also occasionally burrow into loose soils.  Active and alert, they rely on their eyesight and speed to capture prey and evade predators.  Lizards, reptile eggs, and rodents probably compose the majority of their diet, but snakes and insects are also recorded as prey.

Big Bend Patchnose Snakes are fairly slender in build.  [The common name is inspired by the distinctively enlarged rostral scale](https://snakeevolution.org/Sdeserticola.jpg), which apparently aids in excavating loose soil to locate prey.  The dorsal scales are smooth and arranged in 17 rows at midbody.  [There are nine supralabial scales and usually two of those (usually 5,6) contact the eye.  A single loreal scale is present and there are usually two (1-3) preoculars.](https://snakeevolution.org/Sdeserticola.jpg)  The anal scale is divided.  The ground color is pale grey, tan, yellowish, or olive.  A pale tan, yellow, or orange-brown middorsal stripe is widest at the nape but narrows posteriorly.  [This pale stripe is usually the same width or wider than a pair of dark, dorsolateral stripes which border it on each side.  These dark dorsolateral stripes run almost to the eye or through it toward the snout.  An additional pair of dark lateral stripes are much thinner and positioned on the fourth (sometimes rows 3-4) dorsal scale row at midbody.](https://snakeevolution.org/Sdeserticola.jpg)  The crown of the head is slightly darker than but continuous with the middorsal stripe.

Other snakes may be confused for *S. deserticola*.  Texas Patchnose Snakes *S. lineata* and Baird's Patchnose Snake *S. bairdi* usually have eight supralabials, the dark lateral stripes are positioned on the third dorsal scale row, and the pale middorsal stripe is narrower than the dark stripes which flank it.  Mountain Patchnose Snakes *S. grahamiae* usually have eight supralabials and usually lack the dark lateral stripes, but if they are present, they do not involve the fourth dorsal scale row.  Where its range overlaps with *S. deserticola* Western Patchnose Snakes *S. hexalepis* usually have 2-4 loreal scales and only the sixth supralabial contacts the eye.  Mexican Patchnose Snakes *S. mexicana* usually have one preocular and supralabials 4-6 contact the eye.

*Thamnophis* Gartersnakes have strongly keeled dorsal scales, lack the enlarged rostral scale, and have an undivided anal scale.  *Masticophis* Whipsnakes lack both the enlarged rostral scale and the pale middorsal stripe.
