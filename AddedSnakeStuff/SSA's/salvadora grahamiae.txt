Mountain Patchnose Snakes *Salvadora grahamiae* are medium sized (51-76 cm, up to 95cm) colubrid snakes that range from central Arizona and northern New Mexico south into western Texas and northern Coahuila, northern Chihuahua, northern Sonora, MX, from about 850m above sea level up to 2,224m.  They inhabit a wide variety of dry, rocky terrain, including montane woodland and forest, scrubland, grassland, arroyos, rocky slopes, plateaus, and mesas.

Primarily diurnal and terrestrial in habit, *S. grahamiae* are good climbers and may occasionally be found in shrubs and low trees basking or foraging for prey.  Their diet is composed largely of lizards, reptile eggs, and rodents, but snakes and small birds are occasionally consumed.

The dorsal scales are smooth and arranged in 17 rows at midbody.  [The common name is inspired by the distinctively enlarged rostral scale](https://snakeevolution.org/Sgrahamiae.jpg), which apparently aids in excavating loose soil to locate prey.  The dorsal scales are smooth and arranged in 17 rows at midbody.  [There are usually eight (8-9) supralabial scales, with two (usually supralabials 4,5; sometimes 5,6) in contact with the eye.  A single loreal scale is present and there are usually two (1-3) preoculars.](https://snakeevolution.org/Sgrahamiae.jpg)  The anal scale is divided.  The ground color is pale grey, cream, tan, yellowish, or olive.  [A pale grey, tan, or yellow middorsal stripe is widest at the nape but narrows posteriorly.  This pale stripe is usually the same width or wider than a pair of dark, dorsolateral stripes which border it on each side.  Though usually absent, an additional pair of thin, dark, lateral stripes are indistinct and positioned on the third dorsal scale row when present](https://snakeevolution.org/Sgrahamiae.jpg).  The crown of the head is slightly darker than but continuous with the middorsal stripe.

Other snakes may be confused for *S. grahamiae*.  Texas Patchnose Snakes *S. lineata* have very distinct dark stripes positioned on the third dorsal scale row and, at midbody, the pale middorsal stripe is narrower than the dark stripes which flank it.  Big Bend Patchnose Snakes *S. deserticola* usually have nine supralabial scales and the dark lateral stripes are positioned on the fourth scale row anteriorly and at midbody.  Western Patchnose Snakes *S. hexalepis* usually have nine supralabial scales, divided loreal scales, the dark lateral stripes are usually positioned on dorsal scale rows 3-4 at midbody, and where their range overlaps with *S. grahamiae*, usually only one supralabial (the sixth) contacts the eye.  Baird's Patchnose Snake *S. bairdi* have dark stripes that terminate at the neck, distinct dark lateral stripes and, at midbody, the pale middorsal stripe is narrower than the dark dorsolateral stripes which flank it.

*Thamnophis* Gartersnakes have strongly keeled dorsal scales, lack the enlarged rostral scale, and have an undivided anal scale.  *Masticophis* Whipsnakes lack both the enlarged rostral scale and the pale middorsal stripe.
