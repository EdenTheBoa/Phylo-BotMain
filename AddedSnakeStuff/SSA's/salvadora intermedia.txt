Oaxacan Patchnose Snakes *Salvadora intermedia* are medium sized (up to 115cm) colubrid snakes endemic to Mexico, ranging from eastern Guerrero east into southern Puebla and northern Oaxaca, from 500m above sea level up to 2,700m.  They inhabit montane forest, savanna, and arid scrubland.

Primarily diurnal in habit, *S. intermedia* are active and alert, relying on their eyesight and speed to capture prey and evade predators.  Though mainly terrestrial, they are strong climbers and will occasionally ascend shrubs and low trees to bask or to forage for their prey, which consists of lizards, snakes, reptile eggs, and rodents.

Oaxacan Patchnose Snakes are fairly slender in build.  [The common name is inspired by the distinctively enlarged rostral scale](https://snakeevolution.org/Sintermedia.jpg), which apparently aids in excavating loose soil to locate prey.  The dorsal scales are smooth and arranged in 17 rows at midbody.  [There are usually eight (8-9) supralabial scales and two (usually 4,5) contact the eye.  A single loreal scale is present and there are two preoculars.](https://snakeevolution.org/Sintermedia.jpg)  The anal scale is divided.  The ground color is pale grey, cream, tan, yellow, or yellow-orange, and is mainly confined to the lateral surface.  A pale yellow, orange, or cream middorsal stripe is widest at the nape but narrows posteriorly.  This pale stripe is narrower than a pair of dark, dorsolateral stripes which border it on both sides and dominate the overall pattern.  An additional pair of dark lateral stripes are much thinner and positioned on the third dorsal scale row, but may be indistinct in some individuals.  [The dark lateral and dorsolateral stripes merge around the forebody or neck, continuing anteriorly to the eye or through it toward the snout](https://snakeevolution.org/Sintermedia.jpg).  The crown of the head is similar in color or a few shades darker than the middorsal stripe and the two are more or less contiguous.

Other snakes may be confused for *S. intermedia*.  The dark stripes of Baird's Patchnose Snakes *S. bairdi* terminate at the neck rather than continue to or through the eye.  Pacific Patchnose Snakes *S. lemniscata* and Mexican Patchnose Snakes *S. mexicana* have nine supralabials, supralabials 4-6 in contact with the eye, and only one preocular.  Mixe Patchnose Snakes *S. gymnorhachis* lack a pale middorsal stripe and the dark dorsolateral stripes are only present on the forebody.  *Thamnophis* Gartersnakes have strongly keeled dorsal scales, lack the enlarged rostral scale, and have an undivided anal scale.  *Masticophis* Whipsnakes lack both the enlarged rostral scale and the pale middorsal stripe.  *Conophis* Road Guarders are distinctively more robust in form and lack both the enlarged rostral scale and the pale middorsal stripe.  *Rhadinaea* Littersnakes lack the enlarged rostral scale and reach significantly smaller adult sizes.
