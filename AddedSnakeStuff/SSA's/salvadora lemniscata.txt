Pacific Patchnose Snakes *Salvadora lemniscata* are medium-large (110-140cm, up to 162cm) colubrid snakes that range from Guerrero, MX south and east into Chiapas and extreme western Guatemala, from near sea level up to 1,200m.  They inhabit dry, rocky, and well vegetated areas including tropical dry forest, scrubland, and rocky slopes.

Primarily diurnal in habit, *S. lemniscata* are active and alert, relying on their eyesight and speed to capture prey and evade predators.  Though mainly terrestrial, they are strong climbers and will occasionally ascend shrubs and low trees to bask or to forage.  Their only recorded prey are lizards, but it is likely their full diet resembles other species of *Salvadora* and likely includes snakes, reptile eggs, rodents, and possibly other small animals.

Pacific Patchnose Snakes are fairly slender in build.  [The common name is inspired by the distinctively enlarged rostral scale](https://snakeevolution.org/Slemniscata.jpg), which apparently aids in excavating loose soil to locate prey.  The dorsal scales are smooth and arranged in 17 rows at midbody.  [There are nine supralabial scales and three (supralabials 4,5,6) contact the eye.  A single loreal scale is present and there is one preocular.](https://snakeevolution.org/Slemniscata.jpg)  The anal scale is divided.  The ground color is pale grey, cream, tan, yellow, or olive.  A pale middorsal stripe, similar to the ground color, is widest at the neck but narrows toward midbody.  [This pale stripe is flanked by a pair of dark, dorsolateral stripes which are narrow at the neck and become wider toward midbody.  An additional pair of dark lateral stripes are also narrow at the neck but become wider toward midbody.  The dark dorsolateral stripes terminate at the neck, but the lower lateral stripes sometimes merge with a dark stripe behind the eye.](https://snakeevolution.org/Slemniscata.jpg)  The crown of the head is similar to the dorsal ground color, but with dark pigment in the sutures between the scales.

Other snakes may be confused for *S. lemniscata*.  Mexican Patchnose Snakes *S. mexicana* have dark bars or bands which interrupt the dark stripes anteriorly.  All other *Salvadora* Patchnose Snakes that overlap *S. lemniscata* usually have eight supralabials, usually only two supralabials (4-5) in contact with the eye, and two preocular scales.  *Thamnophis* Gartersnakes have strongly keeled dorsal scales, lack the enlarged rostral scale, and have an undivided anal scale.  *Masticophis* Whipsnakes lack both the enlarged rostral scale and the pale middorsal stripe.  *Conophis* Road Guarders are distinctively more robust in form and lack both the enlarged rostrals scale and the pale middorsal stripe.  *Rhadinaea* Littersnakes lack the enlarged rostral scale and reach significantly smaller adult sizes.
