Green Ratsnakes *Senticolis triaspis* are medium-large (70-120cm, up to 183cm) colubrid snakes that range from extreme southern Arizona, USA south into Costa Rica, from near sea level to 2,425m.  They utilize a wide variety of habitat, including savanna, scrubland, woodland, and rainforest.

Primarily nocturnal, *S. triaspis* are usually observed at night while foraging or crossing roads.  Terrestrial in habit, they are good climbers and sometimes found in shrubs and low tree branches.  Their diet consists largely of rodents, but bats, lizards, and small birds are also consumed.

Green Ratsnakes are moderately slender and muscular in build.  [The head](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/163757627?size=large) is distinct at the neck and moderately elongate with large eyes that are in contact with two of the usually eight supralabial scales.  A single loreal, one preocular, and usually two (2-3) postocular scales are present.  The dorsal scales are weakly keeled at middorsum, smooth laterally, and arranged in 27-39 rows at midbody.  

[Juvenile *S. triaspis*](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/129883767?size=large) are light in coloration with 58+ darker dorsal blotches.  These blotches are edged with a thin, black border and some blotches sometimes merge with others, creating a "wavy" or "zig-zag" look.  Adults in the Yucatan Penisula retain this pattern.  In the remainder of their range, the pattern usually fades with age, leaving most adults nearly patternless and various shades of [green](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/67431605), grey, tan, or [brownish](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/248678912?size=large).  Like other North American ratsnakes, the body has a unique cross-sectional shape.  It is slightly compressed laterally, rounded at the top, and angular at the junction of the belly and the sides, both of which are almost flat.  This shape is best likened to a [loaf of bread](https://decoratedtreats.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/homemade-white-bread-loaf-feature-500x500.jpg), a [train tunnel](https://thumbs.dreamstime.com/b/old-train-tunnel-railway-mountain-76646034.jpg), or a [rural mailbox](https://i5.walmartimages.com/asr/a845f7ea-2173-49af-a559-70b2145f8704_1.75c8e9b5192cd187148446aa89e91821.jpeg).

Yucatan Ratsnakes are most likely to be confused with close relatives.  *Pseudelaphe* ratsnakes usually have 9-10 supralabials with three in contact with the eye, usually a proportionally larger eye, and usually fewer than 45 dorsal blotches before the tail (usually 45+ for *S. triaspis*).
