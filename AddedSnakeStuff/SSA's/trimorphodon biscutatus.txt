Western Lyresnakes *Trimorphodon biscutatus* are medium-large (80-110cm, up to 185cm) colubrid snakes that range from western Jalisco, MX south and west to Chiapas and extreme western Guatemala, from near sea level up to 1500m.  They principally inhabit dry, rocky, areas within tropical dry forest, montane woodland, thornscrub, semidesert scrub, and arid grassland.

Rear-fanged snakes, *T. biscutatus* produce a mild venom that helps them subdue small prey items.  They are considered harmless to humans but prolonged, chewing bites should be avoided as a precaution.  Primarily nocturnal, they are strong climbers and often forage in trees and bushes for their prey, which includes lizards, rodents, bats, and small birds.

Western Lyresnakes are fairly slender to moderate in build.  The head is distinctive at the neck, with protruding eyes and elliptical pupils.  Dorsal coloration is light grey, grey-brown, or yellow brown, patterned with 14-30 [dark brown or black blotches](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/247899302?size=large) that have a lighter center and sometimes are completely disconnected at the middle.  The dorsal blotches are irregular in shape, often like the letter H, especially on the neck and forebody, with irregular dark spots distributed in between the blotches.  A [distinctive, dark marking on the head](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/234969?size=large) might be shaped like a V, a pair of batwings, or a lyre, inspiring this species' common name.  A dark bar also runs in between (or just in front of) the eyes.  The dorsal scales are smooth and arranged in 22-28 rows at midbody.

Western Lyresnakes are sometimes confused with other species.  Sinaloan Lyresnakes *T. paucimaculatus* don't have H-shaped dorsal blotches, and lack dark spots in between the blotches on the upper dorsal surface.  Mexican Lyresnakes *T. tau* have a distinctive pale band on the neck.  *Pituophis* Gophersnakes lack the distinctive marking on top of the head, have keeled dorsal scales, at least 27 dorsal scale rows at midbody, and are more robust in build.  Green Ratsnakes *Senticolis triaspis* have an elongate head, 31+ dorsal scale rows, dorsal blotches which do not reach the lateral surface, and adults are usually greenish with no pattern.
