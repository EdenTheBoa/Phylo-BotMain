Sinaloan Lyresnakes *Trimorphodon paucimaculatus* are medium sized (75-100cm, up to 130cm) colubrid snakes that range from Sinaloa, MX south into Jalisco and possibly Colima, from near sea level up to 1100m.  They principally inhabit dry, rocky, woodland including Sinaloan thornscrub and tropical dry forest.

Rear-fanged snakes, *T. paucimaculatus* produce a mild venom that helps them subdue small prey items.  They are considered harmless to humans but prolonged, chewing bites should be avoided as a precaution.  Primarily nocturnal, they are strong climbers and often forage in trees and bushes for their prey, which includes lizards and bats.  Other small animals, such as rodents, birds, and snakes, are probably also consumed.

Sinaloan Lyresnakes are fairly slender in build.  The head is distinctive at the neck, with protruding eyes and elliptical pupils.  Dorsal coloration is light brown or light grey, patterned with 17-31 (average 21) elongate, [darker brown or reddish brown blotches](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/251353523?size=large) that have a lighter center and sometimes are completely disconnected at the middle.  A [distinctive, dark marking on the head](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/10851463?size=large) might be shaped like a V, a pair of batwings, or a lyre, inspiring this species' common name.  A dark bar also runs in between (or just in front of) the eyes.  The dorsal scales are smooth and arranged in 22-27 rows at midbody.

Sinaloan Lyresnakes are sometimes confused with other species.  Sonoran Lyresnakes *T. lambda* average a higher number (18-31, average 24) of dorsal blotches which are shorter in length.  In most areas they can be differentiated on the basis of range, but their exact distributional limitations are poorly resolved.  In these contact zones, differentiation can be difficult.  Western Lyresnakes *T. biscutatus*, can usually be differentiated by having oddly shaped dorsal blotches which often resemble the letter 'H', and also by usually having dark spots present dorsally in between the dark blotches.  Mexican Lyresnakes *T. tau* have a distinctive pale band on the neck.

Glossy Snakes *Arizona elegans* lacks the distinctive V shaped marking on top of the head, they have at least 25 dorsal scale rows at midbody, and the head is comparatively elongate.  *Pituophis* Gophersnakes lack the distinctive marking on top of the head, have keeled dorsal scales, at least 27 dorsal scale rows at midbody, and are more robust in build.  Green Ratsnakes *Senticolis triaspis* have an elongate head, 31+ dorsal scale rows, dorsal blotches which do not reach the lateral surface, and adults are usually greenish with no pattern.
