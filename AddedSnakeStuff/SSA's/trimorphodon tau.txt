Mexican Lyresnakes *Trimorphodon tau* are medium sized (61-91cm, usually under 100cm) colubrid snakes that range from Sonora and Nuevo Leon, MX, southwest to Oaxaca, but absent from most of the Mexican Plateau, from 100m above sea level up to 2600m.  They principally inhabit dry areas, especially rocky slopes within montane woodland, tropical dry forest, savanna, and scrubland.

Rear-fanged snakes, *T. tau* produce a mild venom that helps them subdue small prey items.  They are considered harmless to humans but prolonged, chewing bites should be avoided as a precaution.  Primarily nocturnal, they are most commonly observed at night while crossing roads which bisect their habitat.  Their favorite prey is lizards, but rodents, bats, snakes, and small birds are commonly consumed as well.

Mexican Lyresnakes are fairly slender in build.  The head is distinctive at the neck, with protruding eyes and elliptical pupils.  Dorsal coloration is tan, light brown, or light grey, patterned with 18-46 (average 34) [darker brown or black blotches](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/63333298?size=large) or [transverse bands](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/27126321?size=large).  These dark blotches/bands approach and often reach the edges of the belly, and they also sometimes have a lighter patch in the center.  A distinctive [pale band on the neck](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/213164656?size=large) is immediately followed by a dark dorsal band or blotch, the two often contrasting sharply.  The dorsal scales are smooth and arranged in 17-25 rows at midbody.

Mexican Lyresnakes are sometimes confused with other species.  Other *Trimorphodon* Lyresnakes lack the distinctive pale band on the neck.  Glossy Snakes *Arizona elegans* lacks the distinctive V shaped marking on top of the head, they have at least 25 dorsal scale rows at midbody, and the head is comparatively elongate.  *Pituophis* Gophersnakes lack the distinctive marking on top of the head, have keeled dorsal scales, at least 27 dorsal scale rows at midbody, and are more robust in build. *Lampropeltis* Kingsnakes lack the distinctive pale band on the neck, are more thickset in the body, and will usually have some bright reddish markings dorsally.
