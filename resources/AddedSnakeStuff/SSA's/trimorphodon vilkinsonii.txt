Texas Lyresnakes *Trimorphodon vilkinsonii* are medium sized (46-76cm, up to 104cm) colubrid snakes that range from southern New Mexico and west Texas south into Chihuahua and Coahuila, MX, from between 900 and 1850m above sea level.  They principally inhabit the dry, rocky, pine-oak woodland that characterizes the mountains of the Chihuahuan Desert, but also occur in adjacent ravines, canyons, and basins.

Rear-fanged snakes, *T. vilkinsonii* produce a mild venom that helps them subdue small prey items.  They are considered harmless to humans but prolonged, chewing bites should be avoided as a precaution.  Primarily nocturnal, they are most commonly observed at night while crossing roads which bisect their habitat.  Their favorite prey is lizards, but rodents, bats, snakes, and small birds are also consumed.  They are strong climbers and will sometimes forage in trees and bushes.

Texas Lyresnakes are fairly slender in build.  The head is distinctive at the neck, with protruding eyes and elliptical pupils.  Dorsal coloration is light brown or light grey, patterned with (usually 24 or fewer, average 21) [dark brown blotches which are wider than they are long](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/11551186?size=large), transversely narrowing (often to a point) toward the lateral surface, and with only a small or no light patch in the center.  The markings on the crown of the head, which are so distinctive in related species, are absent or reduced to [a few small spots](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/168279?size=large) or [small dark V on the parietals](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/189799?size=original).  The dorsal scales are smooth and arranged in 21-25 rows at midbody and the anal plate is divided.

Texas Lyresnakes are sometimes confused with other species.  Sonoran Lyresnakes *T. lambda* usually have a higher number of dorsal blotches (18-31, average 24), larger and more prominent light patches in the center of the dark dorsal blotches, and a large, distinctive V-shaped marking on the crown of the head.  In most areas they can be differentiated on the basis of range, but their exact distributional limitations are poorly resolved, and they are reported to hybridize where their ranges contact.  In these contact zones, differentiation can be difficult.

Other snakes are occasionally confused with *T. vilkinsonii* as well.  Glossy Snakes *Arizona elegans* have more numerous dorsal blotches which do not reach the lateral surface or narrow to a point, at least 25 dorsal scale rows at midbody, and the head is comparatively elongate.  *Pituophis* Gophersnakes have keeled dorsal scales, at least 27 dorsal scale rows at midbody, and are more robust in build.  Grey-Banded Kingsnakes *Lampropeltis alterna* have an undivided anal scale, are proportionally thicker in build, and usually have irregularly shaped blotches or bands with some smaller spots in between.  Chihuahuan Nightsnakes *Hypsiglena jani* reach smaller adult sizes, their dark dorsal blotches are smaller and do no extend to the lateral surface, and 2-3 distinctive dark blotches on the neck (sometimes merged into a single, broad band) often connect to dark stripes that run behind the eye.
